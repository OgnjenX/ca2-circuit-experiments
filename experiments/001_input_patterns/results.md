# Results

The primary CA1 spike-count accuracy was 50% in all nominal conditions and every
tested sensitivity and unseen-input case. Cortical input alone produced no CA1
pyramidal spikes; direct CA3 drive produced nearly uniform counts. This is a null
result for this readout under these conditions, not evidence that CA2 has no role.

In the nominal circuit, adding CA3 coincided with a fall in mean CA2 pyramidal
response from about 2,475 to 244 spikes per trial. All four inhibitory populations
became more active. Halving the fitted inhibitory multiplier changed suppression
to an approximately 26% increase in the sensitivity network. The direction is
conditional on fitted inhibition.

Voltage sometimes distinguished the inputs. In the smaller unseen pattern change
without CA3, intact, blocked and scrambled voltage accuracies were 100%, 95% and
55%. The intact-minus-scrambled paired difference was +45 percentage points
(descriptive network interval +35 to +50); intact-minus-blocked was +5 (0 to +15).
Preserved inhibitory streams can carry information. Scrambling may interfere
with that information, but the mechanism was not isolated by an inhibitory-stream
intervention. Larger pattern changes and the new CA3 delays did not retain this
consistent advantage.

Seven of eight additional numerical spot checks met tolerance. With inhibitory
gain 150 and no CA3, intact CA1 basket counts changed from 39 to 36 at higher
precision, exceeding the 5% tolerance. The case remains unresolved. Voltage
effects also depend on parameters and numerical precision.

The downstream biological check was not matched to slice conditions and could
not validate the intended subthreshold EPSP response. No recorded behavioral
task, learning process or social-recognition mechanism was modeled.

The study identifies conditional circuit behavior and assumptions to test. It
does not establish a biological function of CA2 or a new observation in animals.
